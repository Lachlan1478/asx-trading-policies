1. 
Purpose of Policy 
(a) 
Integrity is a core value of Objective Corporation Limited (Objective). Everyone at Objective 
is expected to demonstrate integrity when dealing with shares, options, debentures 
(including convertible notes) and other securities (collectively, Securities) of Objective.  
 
(b) 
The purpose of this Securities Trading Policy (Policy) is to: 
 
 
outline the prohibitions imposed by law on a person trading in Objective’s Securities 
when that person is in possession of price sensitive information regarding Objective 
that is not generally available to the market; and  
 
 
summarise the procedures to be followed by the directors, officers and employees of 
Objective and its subsidiaries (collectively, the Objective Group) when dealing with 
Objective’s Securities.    
 
(c) 
The laws governing the ability of ‘Insiders’ to buy and sell Securities are called 'insider 
trading rules', and a breach of these rules can be a crime that can be the subject of a prison 
sentence. Basically, no-one is allowed to buy or sell Objective’s Securities when they have 
material, price sensitive information about Objective that is not known to the investing 
'public'. 
2. 
Core Concepts 
(a) 
Conduct prohibited by Law: The Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) (Corporations Act): 
 
(i) 
prohibits the trading of Securities based on ‘inside information’ – i.e. information not 
generally known to the market that, if it was known publicly, would likely have a 
material impact on the price of shares (Inside Information), as such term is further 
described in paragraph 2(b) below;  
(ii) 
prohibits a person in possession of Inside Information arranging for someone else to 
do the trading, and/or sharing Inside Information with other people so that those 
other people can gain a benefit; and 
(iii) 
prohibits directors and executives from using information gained in their capacity for 
their own advantage.  
(b) 
What is ‘Inside Information’: This is information about Objective or another company: 
 
(i) 
that is not generally available to the investing public. If information has been 
announced to the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX) so that members of the 
investing public can all access it equally it can be assumed to be generally available. If

(ii) 
if the information were generally available, a reasonable person would expect it to

(iii) 
a person knows, or ought reasonably to know, that the information is not generally

A reasonable person would be taken to expect information to have a material effect on the 
price or value of the Securities of another company, if the information would, or would be 
likely to, influence a person who commonly deals in Securities to either deal or not deal in 
the Securities in any way. 
Examples of Inside Information include (but is not limited to): 
 
A major new customer or supplier contract that would lead to a significant change in 
the scale of Objective’s activities; 
 
 
A material acquisition or divestment, joint venture or merger; 
 
 
A termination of a major contract or a material variation to it; 
 
 
Material departure of reported earnings from forecasts or market expectations; 
 
 
Changes to Objective’s Board of Directors (Board); 
 
 
Giving or receiving a takeover notice; 
 
 
Appointment of a liquidator, administrator or receiver; or 
 
 
Significant legal proceedings against Objective. 
 
(c) 
Who is an ‘insider’:  
 
Any person who possesses Inside Information is an ‘insider’ (i.e. you do not have to 
be an employee, officer or director of the particular company concerned).  
 
 
Objective acknowledges that from time-to-time, its directors, members of the 
executive leadership team, senior managers and employees may, in the course of 
their duties, become aware of Inside Information in respect of Objective.  
 
 
Objective also acknowledges that sometimes the same people, carrying out their 
same duties, might become aware of Inside Information in respect of other entities, 
including Objective customers or suppliers with whom Objective is negotiating a deal.



(d) 
What is ‘Insider Trading’: Insider Trading occurs when a person, in possession of Inside 
Information about Objective or some other company: 
(i) 
buys or sells any of Objective’s Securities or the Securities of some other company; 
or 
(ii) 
procures another person to deal in Objective’s Securities or the securities of some 
other company; or 
(iii) 
directly or indirectly communicates any Inside Information to another person for that 
person's own personal gain by dealing in Objective’s Securities or the Securities of 
another company.  
3. 
Restrictions on Trading  
(a) 
General prohibition: Objective requires all its personnel – directors, executive leadership 
team members, senior manager and all employees and contractors – not to trade in 
Objective’s Securities at any point in time where the person involved has knowledge which 
constitutes Inside Information. This requirement also includes a restriction not to trade in 
another company’s Securities where the person has obtained Inside Information in relation 
to negotiations that Objective may be involved in with that other company. 
(b) 
Non-Trading Periods for Designated Officers:  
(i) 
Additional restrictions apply to Objective’s directors and certain employees 
(Designated Officers). The Designated Officers are: 
 
The Directors of each entity within the Objective Group (including executive 
directors and non-executive directors); 
 
 
Objective’s Chief Executive Officer (CEO); 
 
 
Objective’s Chief Financial Officer (CFO); 
 
 
Objective’s Company Secretary;  
 
 
Objective’s Chief Legal Officer and all in-house lawyers; 
 
 
members of Objective’s Executive Leadership Team (ELT); 
 
 
Key Management Personnel (as defined in the Corporations Act); and 
 
 
any persons designated by Objective’s Company Secretary in writing,

 
are not in possession of Inside Information (as the Insider Trading prohibitions 
continue to apply at all times); and  
 
 
follow the Notification Requirements set out in paragraphs 3(c) and 3(d) below.  
 
(c) 
Designated Officer Prior Notification:  
(i) 
Where a Designated Officer intends to trade in Objective’s Securities: 
 
 
The Designated Officer is required first to notify Objective’s CFO and Company 
Secretary (and if the Designated Officer is either Objective’s CFO or Company 
Secretary then the notification must be given to Objective’s CEO); and   
 
 
All notifications must be in writing (this includes email) and provide Objective’s 
CFO and Company Secretary with at least 7 days’ notice of the proposed trade. 
 
(ii) 
The person who receives the notice of intention to buy or sell shares, is not 
authorised to prevent a Designated Officer from trading, but will provide guidance as 
to whether or not, in their opinion, the Designated Officer should trade at that 
particular time. Objective (via its Company Secretary) requires subsequent notification 
confirming the trading that has occurred. Should the Designated Officer choose to

(d) Designated Officer Subsequent Notification: Designated Officers must notify 
Objective’s CFO and Company Secretary (and if the person is either Objective’s CFO or

(e) 
No procurement of others to trade: All Objective personnel – directors, executive 
leadership team members, senior manager and all employees and contractors – are 
reminded that it is inappropriate to procure others to buy or sell shares in Objective (e.g. 
friends, associates) when the person is precluded from buying or selling Objective’s 
Securities under this Policy, and are also reminded of the need to enforce confidentiality 
against external advisers. 
(f) 
Avoidance of short-term trading: All Objective personnel – directors, executive 
leadership team members, senior manager and all employees and contractors – should 
avoid short-term buying and selling of Objective’s Securities. Please note that transactions 
related to the exercise of options on Objective’s Securities under any employee incentive 
scheme are not considered “short-term trading” for the purposes of this prohibition. 
(g) 
Short-term buying and selling prohibition for Designated Officers: Designated 
Officers are not permitted to buy and sell Objective’s Securities within any 3 month period, 
unless approval is provided by the Board in writing. 
(h) 
Loan security general prohibition: All Objective personnel – directors, executive 
leadership team members, senior manager and all employees and contractors – are 
prohibited, at any time, from granting, directly or indirectly, any form of security (e.g. 
mortgage, charge, pledge or other type of security) over any of Objective’s Securities where 
the shares or rights to shares have yet to vest or be issued under any share or option 
incentive plan offered by Objective.  
(i) 
Loan security extra prohibition: Designated Officers are not permitted to use any of 
Objective’s Securities as follows: 
(i) 
as security in any loan arrangement where Objective’s shares or rights to shares have 
yet to vest or be issued to the Designated Officer under any share or option incentive 
plan offered by Objective;  
(ii) 
in any margin loan, unless prior approval is provided by the CEO or Board in writing; 
or 
(iii) 
in any share lending arrangement, unless prior approval is provided by the Board in 
writing.

approval is provided by the Board in writing.  
4. 
Exceptional Circumstances 
(a) 
A Designated Officer may be approved to trade in Objective’s Securities during a Non-
Trading Period, or other dealing in Objective’s Securities may be approved that would 
otherwise be prohibited by this Policy, on the basis that the trade is required due to 
exceptional circumstances, provided that the Designated Officer satisfies the following 
requirements: 
(i) 
The Designated Officer is not in possession of any Insider Information about 
Objective;  
 
(ii) 
The Designated Officer applies to Objective’s CFO and Company Secretary for 
approval to trade and satisfies Objective’s CFO and Company Secretary that an 
exceptional circumstance has arisen and the trade is the only reasonable course of 
action to take (in which case Objective’s CFO and Company Secretary will raise the 
matter to Objective’s CEO or the Board for consideration); and 
 
(iii) 
Objective’s CEO or the Board agrees that an exceptional circumstance applies and 
gives prior approval for the trade in writing (email included), 
 
provided that when the applicable Designated Officer under this Paragraph 4(a) is 
Objective’s CEO, then each reference to ‘Objective’s CEO’ in sub-paragraphs (ii) and (iii) 
above shall be construed as deleted from those sub-paragraphs (so that only the Board may 
approve). 
 
(b) 
‘Exceptional circumstances’ will be assessed on a case-by-case basis and may include: 
(i) 
Severe financial hardship: a pressing financial commitment or tax liability that can only 
be satisfied by selling the relevant Objective’s Securities. Please note that a tax liability 
relating to Objective’s Securities received under an employee incentive scheme would 
also not normally constitute severe financial hardship;  
 
(ii) 
Court Order: a requirement to trade in Objective’s Securities as a result of: (a) a court 
order; (b) court enforceable undertakings (e.g. as part of a bona fide family 
settlement); or (c) some other overriding legal or regulatory requirement; or  
 
(iii) 
Other exceptional circumstances: other circumstances considered genuinely 
exceptional by the board.

5. 
Risk  
(a) 
Even if an exception set out in this Policy applies to a trade in Objective’s Securities, or 
approval has been given as required, the person who does the trading bears the legal risk of 
compliance at all times with applicable law, including (without limitation) the Corporations 
Act.  
(b) 
Any Objective determination or approval should not be taken as an endorsement of any 
trade.   
6. 
ASX Notifications 
(a) 
Objective must notify the ASX within 5 business days after any change to a director's 
relevant interest in Objective’s Securities or any entity within the Objective Group, including 
whether the change occurred inside a Non-Trading Period and, if so, whether prior written 
approval was provided.  
(b) 
To enable Objective to comply with the obligation set out in paragraph 6(a), a director must 
promptly (and no later than 2 business days after any relevant event) notify Objective’s 
Company Secretary in writing of the requisite information for Objective’s Company Secretary 
to make the necessary notifications to the Australian Securities and Investments 
Commission and the ASX (as required under the Corporations Act and ASX Listing Rules). 
7. 
Changes to Policy  
If Objective makes any material changes to this Policy, the amended Policy will be provided to the 
ASX for release to the market within 5 business days of the material changes taking effect.   
8. 
Record Keeping 
Objective’s CFO and Company Secretary will ensure records are kept of all applications by 
Designated Officers for approval under this Policy and decisions made in respect of those 
applications.   
9. 
Breach of Policy 
(a) 
Objective’s duty of compliance with Insider Trading laws is a legal obligation.  
 
(b) 
Any Objective personnel engaged in behaviour that amounts to non-compliance with the 
Corporations Act and/or ASX Listing Rules may expose Objective to the risk of fines, 
personal liability for officers and damage to Objective’s reputation.

10. This Policy is not legal advice   
(a) 
Nothing in this Policy constitutes legal advice, and any persons trading in Objective’s 
Securities do so as individuals responsible for their own trading and compliance with the 
law, including (without limitation) the Corporations Act.  
 
(b) 
If there is any inconsistency between this Policy and the law, the law will prevail. 
11. Reviews 
The Board will review this Policy at least once in each reporting period to ensure that it is operating 
effectively. Any variation to this Policy is subject to Board approval.

This Policy was adopted by the Board on the date on the front cover of this Policy and takes effect 
from that date.
